# A/B: `b1-sound` vs the head it merged (`57fd2da`)

The soundness branch changes what the FLOOR and the CEILING say, so it changes
what the search prefers. This is the cost side of that, per board class, never
pooled.

## Method

Two worktrees, each built with `npx tsc -p .`:

* **before** = `57fd2da` (a clean `git worktree add --detach`, `node_modules`
  symlinked)
* **after** = this branch

`node dist/tests/local-game.js <scenario> 30 <seed> --nodes --json=<file>` for
`scenario` in `mixed, snakes, sparse, potions` and `seed` in `1, 2, 3` — the
deterministic node-budget mode, so a delta is a fact about the build and not
about the machine. Compared with `node scripts/ab-compare.js before after`.
All 24 runs completed; every seed paired.

## What moved

* **sparse: byte-identical.** Every counter is 0 on every seed. A board with
  one unit a side and nothing contested exercises none of the readings this
  branch changed, and it says so.
* **snakes: meals up, deaths up.** `+1.50` meals/100 (2 seeds up, 1 flat) and
  `+0.38` deaths/100 (2 up, 1 flat) — two extra self-deaths across three games.
* **mixed: deaths up `+0.15`/100 on all three seeds**, meals `−0.67`, stationary
  `+2.25`.
* **potions: deaths up `+0.37`/100, meals `−1.80`, stationary `−1.84`,
  potion tier-ups `+0.23`.**

Deaths are up on three of four board classes and down on none. The sign test
over three seeds cannot resolve better than `p = 0.25`, so this is a direction
rather than a result — but it is the direction, and it is the price recorded
for the repair rather than one argued away.

## Why it is the price of the repair, and not a regression to revert

Every change on this branch narrows a bound the evaluator was reading OUTSIDE
its own worlds: a term whose `lo` sat above a world the resolver itself
produces, or whose `hi` sat below one. Measured on the same 240 generated
boards by `src/lobster/evaluate/law-sweep.test.ts`:

| class | before | after |
|---|---:|---:|
| `command.hi` | 600 | 199 |
| `reach.lo` | 128 | 106 |
| `total lo` | 0 | 0 |

A floor that is correct and looser orders candidates differently, and some of
those orderings are worse in a thirty-turn game. The alternative is a floor
that is tighter because it is wrong, which is the one bug class the whole bank
exists to forbid.
