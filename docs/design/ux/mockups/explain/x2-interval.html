B · the three-dot quantile strip

Kay et al.'s quantile dotplot, collapsed to three: one dot at the floor, one at est, one at the ceiling, spaced on the shared scale. Discrete marks are read more accurately than a continuous bar.

REJECTED. A quantile dotplot is a claim about a DISTRIBUTION and this is a bracket: there is no density between the endpoints and drawing three dots invites the reader to interpolate one. It also loses the width, which is the number the operator needs.

REJECTED, and it is the most dangerous of the three. An eval bar is a point estimate, and the point estimate here is est — the one channel that never adjudicates. It draws the three identical-floor rows as three identical bars and hides that the ranking among them is a salted coin-flip.
