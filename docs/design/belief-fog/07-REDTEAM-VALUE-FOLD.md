## 2. The k→1 walk is over-claimed, and the endogeneity is the real exposure

**The walk.** They call k's march 2.92 → 2.07 → 1.23 "the strongest internal
evidence that the three flows are the right three." It is not. As channels
are added, the regression approaches the accounting identity (terminal share
IS initial + net folded flows), so k→1 and R²→1 are guaranteed by
BOOKKEEPING for ANY complete carving of the same ledger — a different
decomposition of the same flows would walk to 1 identically. The walk
evidences completeness, which they do state; it cannot select among complete
bases. What actually distinguishes carvings: the residual's STRUCTURE (is
the remaining 0.03–0.05 sharePar white across rosters/turns, or does it load
on identifiable events?) and out-of-premise transport. Their honest rook
test probes the second; nobody has yet looked at the first — one afternoon
with replays they already have, and it is the cheapest way their basis claim
could DIE, which is what makes it worth running.

**The endogeneity.** Flows are policy-conditional: the fit reads flows
generated by the territory-vs-material pair and maps them to the paired
score delta. Deploying the fold as an evaluator CHANGES the policy that
generates the flows — the constant is then being used at a policy it was not
fitted under, and ruling 49's own words ("bot-vs-bot numbers are potentially
distortionary") name the hazard. The repair is not to refuse the fold — it
is the standing iteration: the first live sweep of a fold-bot re-fits k on
the fold-bot's OWN games and publishes |k_refit − k_fit| as the transport
drift metric. A drift near zero is the finding that the premium is a
game-structural constant; a large drift is the finding that k was a policy
artifact. Either is worth more than the point estimate.

## 3. The wipe-closure defect — "a death costs exactly the balance it wipes" is false twice, by engine rule

Their dissolution argument prices a death at the account balance w_u. Two
engine-verified couplings break the equality in the direction that matters
(underpricing catastrophic outflows):

1. **Regicide.** `ClashKind: "regicide" — removed with its team when the
   team's last king fell` (shared/types/Game.ts, read this session). A last
   king's death wipes the TEAM'S ENTIRE BALANCE. Pricing the king's outflow
   at w_king misses the closure by up to W_team/w_king — an order of
   magnitude on typical mix-king boards. No fitted cell contained a king
   (§1's premise row), so the fit could never notice.
2. **Collector revocation.** A vulnerable (−1) collector's death revokes its
   whole team's +1 buffs the same turn (TeamSnekProcessor expiry coupling,
   pinned and engine-verified in the potion-terms work). The collector's
   outflow closure includes the OPTION VALUE of every live ally window —
   which is precisely the quantity the potion-seek retrodiction found
   predictive (+30pts pickup-to-cut).

**Fix, in their own algebra:** balanceFactor's argument is not w_u but
`w_closure(death event)` — the balance the death event wipes under the
rules: w_u for an ordinary unit, W_team for a last king, w_u plus the
priced window value for a buffed team's collector. Computable from the rules
at evaluation time, no new fit. Their γ then exponentiates the closure, not
the unit weight — which also makes their own "the account wipe is the one
unbounded balance ratio" sentence literally true for kings.

## 4. The cliff inequality does not dissolve — it MOVES to the quantifier/measure boundary

Their §3 claim: denominate safety in weight-share and "there is no cliff to
cross, because a death costs exactly the balance it wipes, which is what
material would have charged. The inequality was a unit-conversion guard, not
a strategy convention."

The check: the shipped cliff is TWO guards fused, and the currency dissolves
exactly one of them.

- **The unit-conversion guard** (ordering terms in cells vs material in
  ×10-weight, needing a hand-set separation): genuinely dissolved. Their
  argument is right and it is the good half of their §3.
- **The soundness guard is not dissolvable, and their own document proves
  it.** Their precedence table keeps `doomed` at lattice bottom with the
  sentence "encoding it as a large negative is exactly what would let a dial
  buy a suicide." That sentence IS the cliff — restated one row above the
  claim that the cliff is gone. What the lattice bottom protects is not a
  unit conversion but a QUANTIFIER: certain death is a fact about every
  world in S, and no expectation under any weight may outbid a
  universally-quantified loss. The moment death is priced as
  `(K/W)(1−s)·w_closure·P(death)` in an additive fold, a large enough
  inflow ESTIMATE outbids a NEAR-certain death — and every estimator on the
  inflow side is optimistic by their own horizon-1 admission (rates are
  estimated; wipes are proved). The failure mode is not hypothetical: it is
  the documented shape "the 40×40 room count exceeded −1000 and inverted
  the cliff", which their currency re-enables at P(death) < 1 with better
  units.

**Where the boundary actually lies (the epistemics statement):** the cliff
survives as the S/w boundary. Death PROVED over all of S → lattice bottom,
outside the fold's domain (they agree). Death POSSIBLE → the probabilistic
mass is priced in the fold — but P(death) is a WEIGHT-dependent advised
quantity, supplied by the D2 socket, and under ruling 13's worst-case
population its envelope is [0,1]: the sound reading of their fold's death
term is the whole closure, and only a non-adversarial weight buys it down.
So the fold's safety channel is exactly as good as the weight supplier —
which means the value fold CONSUMES the epistemics factorization rather than
replacing the guard: their `rate: interval` needs the (horizon, weight-id,
basis) tags of 02-doc, or the fold recreates precision laundering one level
up (an interval rate collapsed to a point by an unfitted efficiency inherits
the fold's arithmetic authority — the same defect core.ts:1545 had, in new
clothes). Same verdict for their potion row: `P(win|tier)` is a D2 output;
under the worst case it is vacuous, and their potion-value formula prices to
zero-or-everything until a weight exists.

**One more collision, flagged for the joint frame:** γ (their
risk-concentration exponent on outflows) is a risk attitude — the expected
share loss is linear in the closure by their own identity, so any γ ≠ 1 is
variance-aversion content. The dof synthesis puts ALL fear in one surface
(D3). γ and ε must not ship as two independent knobs; either γ is derived
(from the metric's convexity at the current standing, computable live) or it
is a D3 parameter swept with ε in the same grid. Two fear dials that
compensate is the exact configuration the joint frame exists to forbid.
