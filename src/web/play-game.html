Keyboard & board shortcuts

Ctrl + / or Esc to close · click outside to dismiss

Selecting a unit

Tab
Cycle through the units you own, in letter rank order (A, B, C…) with your own team first — the same order the units table lists them in.
Esc
Deselect the current unit (and clear any cell inspection).
Click
Click one of your units on the board, or its row in the units table, to select it.

Choosing a move — arrow pad

↑ / W
Extend forward along the current axis, out to the last legal square of the ray.
↓ / S
Retract back toward hold, keeping the axis. Snakes cannot hold or reverse, so theirs stops short.
← → / A D
Turn through the unit’s legal axis ring; switching axis resets the distance to 1.
↑+←/→
Pawns only — hold Up and tap Left/Right for the diagonal-forward candidates. Left/Right alone rotate the pawn.
Space
Stage the selected candidate as this unit’s move for the turn.

Choosing a move — numpad

1…9
Absolute directions. A direction key selects its axis at distance 1 and repeat-presses extend along it.
opposite key
Retracts one square per press, through hold and out the other side — crossing hold flips the axis to the direction of travel.
5
Hold (no change): selects the stay candidate and resets the axis to the unit’s own orientation. This is a candidate for this turn; H below makes standing still a standing order.
pawns
7/9 diagonal-forward, 4/6 rotate, 8 forward, 2 retract to hold.
Works with NumLock either way. An unavailable move flashes the unit’s cell red instead of staging anything.

Commanding a unit

H
Hold — the selected unit stands its ground: it stages a stay every turn, outranking the engine, until you press H again, clear its commands, or give it another one. A shield above its head marks it, live and in replay. Moving costs health and standing still does not, so this is how a piece with nothing worth reaching stops paying for pointless steps. Chess pieces only — a snake’s head has to vacate its square every turn, so it cannot hold.
Del
Clear every command given to the selected unit — staged move, goto queue, near target, staged rotation — reverting it to no human input at all. It then does whatever no input means for it (a snake follows the bot; a piece holds).
Right-click
goto target — a green cell marked with a cross: the bot is rewarded for approaching it. A weighted vote in the same scoring matrix every heuristic feeds — never a path override — so survival can still outvote it. Right-click the lone target again to cancel.
Shift+Right-click
Cue another goto target onto the queue instead of replacing it; shift-right a queued cell again to remove it. Only the active (first) target steers the bot; the faded path is a prediction.
Ctrl+click
near target — a blue cell marked with a ring: rewards staying close without arriving — the bonus peaks one square away. Single target; ctrl-click it again to remove.

The turn

Ctrl+Enter
Submit All — mark every unit you control done for this turn. Binding: confirmed staged moves freeze and the turn can end early.
Enter / Esc
Confirm / cancel an open dialog (take-over, certain-death confirmation). Dialogs take these keys first.

Inspecting the board

Alt+click
Inspect a cell: what is standing on it, and what the lens has to say about it — whether its occupant is still a variable the bot is solving, or is fixed and by whom.
Alt tap
Tap Alt on its own to cycle which unit tags are shown by default: all → our team’s → none. Hovering a unit whose tag is down calls it up; resting on a tag that is up makes it step aside. Persisted per browser. Now: all tags shown.
click
A plain left click on empty board clears the inspection.

History & replay

← →
Scrub one turn back / forward while the scrubber has focus.
arrows / numpad
After clicking a unit or a candidate cell, the same move-selection schema steers the replayed candidates instead; Esc hands the arrows back to the scrubber.
drag
Drag the turn slider, or use |◀ ← → ▶|, to move through the game. Live control keys stay inert while scrubbing.

Inspecting the decision

select a unit
Selecting a unit opens its cluster — the other units the bot is solving jointly with it — and the top movesets for that cluster, given the candidate under your cursor. The candidate column is scored as best-of-cluster: what the whole cluster can do if this unit plays that move, not what the move is worth on its own.
[ ]
Walk down and up the moveset list. The board draws the difference: the members whose move would change light up, the ones that agree keep their arrow and gain a ring. Rank 1 and the foil are drawn at full size; the rest are one line each.
F
The foil — the runner-up drawn in teal, dotted, only where it differs from the row you are on, with the margin and the clause that decided. It is on the panel without asking; F puts it on the board.
B
Drill a member: its contribution, its terms, and the joint residual — the part of the total that is not any single member’s, drawn even when it is zero, because a zero cross term is itself a finding.
Space
Lock. It stages exactly the moveset on screen, by pinning this unit plus every member whose move differs from what is staged — the count is on the affordance before you press. On rank 1 that is one pin, yours, which is precisely what Space always did.
Shift+Space
Lock the whole moveset: pin every member, whatever the bot would have inferred. A lock that pins more than your own unit arms first — the affordance says how many, and the same key again commits it.
U
Undo, and the peer of Space: it takes back the last thing you did to a unit — a lock’s pins, a hold, a staged move — leaving goto and near targets alone. A released unit rejoins the cluster, which widens for everyone looking at it. The chip beside the lock always says what U would undo.
Confirm or undo? Undo for everything reversible — a dialog on a reversible action is a wasted interaction, and on a half-second clock it is a lost turn. Dialogs are kept for what cannot be taken back: Submit All, certain-death consent, and taking a unit over from another operator.

Key scheme

scheme
One action set, three layouts — pick the one your hands already do. Persisted in this browser.
density
The rail’s type scale. compact for a laptop, roomy for a screen at arm’s length.

The turn timeline

, .
Step one event back / forward within the current turn. The playhead snaps to events, never to pixels: a moment between two events is the earlier event’s frame.
Shift+, .
Jump kernel emission to kernel emission — the moments the bot actually changed its mind.
Home End
To the board’s arrival, or to the head.
N
Back to now. Stepping back through the turn is loud on purpose: the ink fades and every determination is refused, because locking against a frame whose ordering has moved would stage something other than what you are looking at.

Display

Ctrl + /
Open or close this pane, anywhere on the page.
unit tags
A unit’s numbers ride on its OWN body — letter on the head, then weight, health, and any buff with the turns it has left, and the tail’s stacked-part count on the tail. A tag appears only for a unit too short to carry all of them (a piece always is), and then obeys the Alt-tap mode above. A long enough snake never shows one.
violet
On the board, violet means hypothetical — a cluster’s tethers and chips, and the moves a moveset implies. Nothing else on the board is violet. A member whose implied move matches what is already staged gets a ring on the existing arrowhead rather than a second arrow: only disagreement draws, so walking the moveset list lights up exactly what would change.
